Signed-in readers can discuss any paragraph of an article. Discussions are public and appear under your name. They are meant to sharpen arguments, raise questions, and point to authorities: the kind of conversation a good seminar has.

## Please

- **Stay on the text.** Respond to the paragraph you're commenting on.
- **Argue the point, not the person.** Disagree as sharply as the reasoning deserves, never with insults, name-calling, or attacks on an author or another reader.
- **Cite when you can.** A case, a statute, or an article makes a comment useful to the next reader.
- **Keep it honest.** Don't post what you know to be false, and don't pretend to be someone else.

## Don't post

- harassment, threats, hate speech, or sexual content;
- personal information about anyone, including confidential details of real cases or clients;
- advertising, spam, or links unrelated to the discussion;
- anything that would breach a court order, the Code of Professional Responsibility and Accountability, or the law.

## How moderation works

Comments appear as soon as they are posted. Any signed-in reader can **Report** a comment, and the Journal's editors review reports. When three readers report the same comment, it is hidden until an editor looks at it. Editors may **hide** or **delete** comments that break these guidelines, and may **mute** a reader who keeps breaking them: a muted reader can still read and keep their library, but can't post for 7 days, 30 days, or until an editor lifts it. Every moderation decision is recorded for the board. You can delete your own comments at any time.

Comments are the views of their writers, not of the Journal, its editors, or the WVSU College of Law. Nothing posted here is legal advice.

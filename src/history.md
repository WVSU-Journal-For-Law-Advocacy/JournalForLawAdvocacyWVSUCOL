The **WVSU Journal for Law Advocacy** is a student publication of the West Visayas State University College of Law in Iloilo City. It was founded in 2021, the year on its seal, as a window for law students to take their legal writing, analysis, and research further, and as a home for "a robust marketplace of ideas."

## How it began

The Journal's genesis is dated **August 21, 2021**. To organize it, the College constituted an **Executive Committee** of law professors under Dean Pauline Grace B. Alfuente, with Judge Enrique Z. Trespeces as adviser, to select the first editors, advise them on their publication agenda, and guard the Journal's integrity.

The Committee chose a deliberately less hierarchical design. Instead of an editor-in-chief, a small Board of Editors and a team of Contribution Editors would share decisions. On **September 6, 2021**, it invited twelve students of the pioneering **Batch Banaag** to a competitive examination, followed by a commentary-writing marathon on political dynasties. On **October 13** the selection was complete: Clyde Ben A. Gacayan, Edson Chase H. Yap, and Kirby V. Aguaras to the Board of Editors, with Clyde Ben A. Gacayan as its first Executive Editor; and Rene M. Aviles, Jr., Clint Deo N. Genciana, Theda Grace S. Gumban, Mike Gabriel G. Guillem, and Vanessa G. Silla as Contribution Editors.

On **November 16, 2021**, the new editors trained with Dr. Mary Barby Badayos-Jover, a former editor of the University of the Philippines Visayas's *Danyag* journal, on academic publishing and peer review, and with Paulo Romeo Yusi, then Editor-in-Chief of the *Philippine Law Journal*, on managing a student-run law journal. They took their oath of office on **December 2, 2021**, committing the Journal to "the development of legal scholarship for and from the Visayas Region and Southern Philippines" and to double-blind peer review.

## The volumes

The call for papers for the inaugural issue, on electoral law, went out in September 2021. Of twelve submissions from law students and legal professionals, seven went through double-blind review and revision, and six were published.

- **Volume 1 (2021–2022)**, "Electoral Laws and the 2022 Philippine Election," was released online chapter by chapter from August 2022, opening with an overview of the national elections written by the founding editors.
- **Volume 2 (2022–2023)** collected eight student essays, from vaccination in the workplace and children in conflict with the law to cyber libel and the case for a jury system. In December 2022 it was printed together with Volume 1 in the Journal's inaugural book.
- The **Second Issue**, on human rights reforms, was launched in December 2023.
- **Volume 4 (December 2024)** took up environmental law.
- **Volume 5 (December 2025)** examined the Bill of Rights in the digital revolution.

Volume 6, now in preparation, takes up public accountability and the fight against corruption. Each year's board also trains through editorial workshops, often led by the Journal's own alumni.

From the start the Journal has had the support of the College of Law's deanship and of its adviser, Judge Enrique Z. Trespeces, who administers the oath to each new board. Atty. Regina Johanna V. Gustilo and Prosecutor Victoria Heler have also served as advisers.

## Organization

The Journal is run by students of the College of Law under the guidance of its faculty Advisorship. Each academic year the Advisorship announces the editorial staff:

- the **Board of Editors**, led by the Executive Editor, which screens, edits, and approves manuscripts;
- the **Contribution Editors**, led by the Executive Contribution Editor, who work with authors on their submissions; and
- the **Editorial Apprentices**, newer members who train in legal editing and research.

The Journal's operations are governed by its Manual of Operations.

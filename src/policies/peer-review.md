The Journal for Law Advocacy follows a two-stage review process.

## 1. Editorial screening

Every manuscript is first read by the Board of Editors. Within **two weeks** of receipt, the Board decides whether the manuscript:

- fits the scope of the journal (scholarship on Philippine law, legal practice, and the public interest);
- meets the formatting and citation requirements in the [submission guidelines](/submit/); and
- passes a similarity check (see [Publication ethics](/policies/publication-ethics/)).

Manuscripts that do not pass screening are returned to the author with reasons.

## 2. Review

Manuscripts that pass screening are sent to at least **one faculty reviewer** of the College of Law or a practitioner with expertise in the subject. Reviews are **double-blind**: the reviewer does not know the author's identity, and the author does not know the reviewer's.

Reviewers recommend one of the following:

- **Accept** as submitted or with minor edits;
- **Revise and resubmit**, with written comments for the author;
- **Reject**, with written reasons.

## 3. Decision and editing

The Board of Editors makes the final decision, taking the reviewer's recommendation into account. Accepted manuscripts go through substantive and technical editing (citations, style, and proofreading) with the author before publication.

## Conflicts of interest

Editors and reviewers withdraw from any manuscript written by someone they supervise, are related to, or have a personal or professional conflict with. Manuscripts written by members of the Editorial Board are handled by other board members and reviewed by a faculty reviewer outside the board.
